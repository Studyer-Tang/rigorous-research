# Literature, openness, and novelty

## Search as a claim audit

Translate the claim into names, notation variants, equivalent formulations, special cases, and broader theories. Search primary databases, cited references, forward citations, author pages, corrections, theses, and recent preprints. Record queries, date, database, and coverage limits.

Use secondary summaries to discover sources, then verify decisive facts in primary texts. “No result found” means only that the documented search did not find one.

## Establishing that a problem was open

Look for a dated source explicitly posing it, later work describing its status, and absence of a known resolution within the search boundary. An old question, an author's recollection, or low community interest does not by itself establish current openness.

## Novelty decomposition

Separate novelty of:

- the theorem statement;
- the interpretation or correction of the statement;
- the proof method;
- the computation or witness;
- exposition or implementation.

A new calculation of a known theorem is not a new theorem. A result under a different interpretation may be valuable but is not automatically a resolution of the original formulation.

## Expert communication

Treat author or expert feedback as evidence about intent, context, or likely errors. It is not peer review unless a substantive review actually occurred. Quote it only with permission when private, distinguish verbatim text from paraphrase, and never imply endorsement from a polite or tentative response.
