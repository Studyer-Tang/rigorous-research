# Claim calibration and publication

## The claim ladder

Use the highest rung fully supported by the audit:

1. exploratory observation;
2. reproducible computation for specified instances;
3. conditional proposition under explicit assumptions;
4. theorem for one labeled interpretation;
5. resolution of the original posed problem;
6. novel resolution of a previously open problem.

Each rung adds obligations. Rung 5 requires scope identity with the original question. Rung 6 additionally requires a credible openness and novelty search.

## Abstract test

Every abstract should answer:

- What exact object and interpretation are used?
- What is proved, and under which parameter assumptions?
- What method supplies the decisive step?
- What is not claimed?

Avoid “solves,” “first,” “breakthrough,” “autonomous,” and “verified” unless the record directly supports them. Describe AI assistance factually where disclosure rules or scholarly norms call for it.

## Release gate

Before public release, require:

- no unresolved critical proof obligations;
- notation and source locators checked against the cited version;
- computations rerunnable from a clean environment or fully specified;
- literature claims qualified to the actual search boundary;
- private correspondence quoted only with permission;
- title and abstract no stronger than the body;
- limitations and alternative interpretations visible, not buried.

If a gate fails, publish only as an exploratory note with the gap explicit, or continue the investigation.
